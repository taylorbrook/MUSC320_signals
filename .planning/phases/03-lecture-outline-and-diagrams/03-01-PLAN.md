**Structure (follow the time allocation from RESEARCH.md Pattern 2):**

Use time-range section headers: `## [00:00-03:00] Section Title (N min)`

**Sections in order:**

1. **[00:00-03:00] Opening: Demo and Download (3 min)**
   - PATCH: `noise-sculpture-demo.maxpat` -- open in Presentation Mode, play with all sliders
   - Script the 30-second wow moment: toggle ON, move sliders, let it evolve
   - Script the QR download moment: "Scan this QR code to download the patches. You should see a folder with 9 files. Thumbs up when you have them."
   - BRIDGE: "You've built Max patches with messages and numbers. Today we enter the world of signals -- continuous streams of audio data."
   - CHECKPOINT after download: confirm students have patches before proceeding
   - Fallback: "If download isn't working, follow along on the projector"

2. **[03:00-08:00] Digital Audio Basics (5 min)**
   - Reference diagram: `digital-audio-waveform.svg` -- project it
   - Explain: sound is a continuous wave, but computers store it as discrete samples
   - Key number: 44,100 samples per second (CD quality)
   - BRIDGE: "In Max, you send individual messages -- a bang, a number. In MSP, data flows as a continuous stream, 44,100 numbers per second. Text messages vs phone call."
   - Introduce the tilde (~): every MSP object has a tilde because it processes signals, not messages
   - Fallback: if running long, compress to 3 min -- just the diagram + "44,100 samples/sec" + tilde convention

3. **[08:00-12:00] Patch 01: Hello Signal (4 min)**
   - PATCH: `01-hello-signal.maxpat` -- highlight ezdac~, cycle~ 220, the output chain
   - Students open the same patch on their machines
   - BRIDGE for ezdac~: "You've been using Max without audio. ezdac~ is the power switch for MSP."
   - BRIDGE for cycle~: "metro generates bangs at regular intervals. cycle~ generates a smooth wave at a frequency you choose -- 220 times per second."
   - Walk through the signal chain: cycle~ -> *~ -> live.gain~ -> clip~ -> dac~
   - Toggle ezdac~ -- hear the tone
   - Fallback: none (never skip -- foundational)

4. **[12:00-15:00] Patch 02: First Tone (3 min)**
   - PATCH: `02-first-tone.maxpat` -- highlight frequency comparison scopes (220/440/880)
   - BRIDGE for *~: "You used * to multiply numbers. *~ multiplies signals -- same math, 44,100 times per second."
   - Point out the three cycle~ frequencies and their scope displays -- octave relationships
   - Quick: this is a fast section, concept is straightforward
   - Fallback: compress to 2 min if needed -- just show the three frequencies, skip amplitude detail

5. **[15:00-22:00] Patch 03: Control Meets Signal (7 min)**
   - PATCH: `03-control-meets-signal.maxpat` -- highlight side-by-side: slider -> number box (left) vs slider -> sig~ -> number~ (right)
   - THE critical concept of the lecture. Front-load time here.
   - BRIDGE for sig~: "sig~ converts a single number into a continuous signal -- 44,100 copies per second flowing through the cord."
   - BRIDGE for number~: "A number box shows one value. number~ shows a signal's value -- but only updates a few times per second because the actual signal changes too fast to read."
   - Move the slider -- both sides update, but explain the fundamental difference
   - Toggle DSP -- hear pitch change as slider moves
   - Brief "try the slider" moment: "Everyone move your slider. Hear the pitch change?"
   - CHECKPOINT: "Can everyone hear the pitch change? Thumbs up."
   - Reference diagram: `signal-flow.svg` -- show abstract signal flow, then the demo's concrete chain
   - Fallback: never skip this section. If running long, skip the signal-flow diagram reference but keep the hands-on slider moment.

6. **[22:00-25:00] Three-Level Comparison (3 min)**
   - PATCH: `three-level-comparison.maxpat` -- show three columns: metro (control), phasor~ (signal), gen~ (sample)
   - Frame before opening: "There's actually a third level of processing in Max. We won't learn it today, but I want you to see it exists."
   - Point out the visual difference: staircase (metro) vs smooth ramp (phasor~) vs smooth ramp (gen~)
   - Key takeaway: "MSP sits between Max messages and gen~ sample-level processing. MSP is where we'll work this semester."
   - Hard time limit: 3 min max -- do not linger. Script the exit: "That's gen~ -- a topic for another day."
   - Fallback: SECOND CUT -- skip entirely if running long (save 3 min). Say: "There's a third level called gen~ -- you'll see a comparison patch in your download. For now, back to building."

7. **[25:00-28:00] Patch 04: Noise and Amplitude (3 min)**
   - PATCH: `04-noise-and-amplitude.maxpat` -- highlight noise~ vs cycle~ comparison scopes
   - BRIDGE for noise~: "random gives you a random number each bang. noise~ gives you a random number every sample -- 44,100 random numbers per second. That's white noise."
   - Show the two scopes: periodic (cycle~) vs aperiodic (noise~)
   - **[TRY THIS - 90 seconds]** "Move the amplitude slider. What happens to the noise? Now compare it with the tone. That's *~ -- multiplying the signal controls its loudness."
   - Fallback: compress to 2 min -- skip the scope comparison, just demo noise~ and the slider

8. **[28:00-32:00] Patch 05: Envelopes (4 min)**
   - PATCH: `05-envelopes.maxpat` -- highlight toggle -> sel -> line~ -> *~ chain
   - BRIDGE for line~: "You used line to smoothly ramp between values for MIDI fades. line~ does the same ramp at audio rate -- smooth enough to shape volume without clicks."
   - Show the envelope scope: watch the fade-in/fade-out shape
   - Toggle on and off -- hear the smooth fade
   - **[TRY THIS - 90 seconds]** "Toggle your patch on and off. Watch the envelope scope. Now imagine if there were no envelope -- the sound would click on and off. line~ is what makes it smooth."
   - Fallback: compress to 2 min -- toggle demo only, skip try-this pause

9. **[32:00-36:00] Patch 06: Modulation and Mixing (4 min)**
   - PATCH: `06-modulation-and-mixing.maxpat` -- highlight phasor~ LFO, +~ voice mixing, master toggle
   - BRIDGE for phasor~: "metro fires bangs at regular intervals. phasor~ generates a smooth 0-to-1 ramp repeating at your frequency. It drives continuous modulation."
   - BRIDGE for +~: "You added numbers with +. +~ adds signals. Adding two sound waves means you hear both -- that's mixing."
   - Point out the multi-voice architecture: two sources mixed via +~
   - Toggle master on -- hear both voices together
   - CHECKPOINT: "We've now covered oscillators, noise, envelopes, modulation, and mixing. Any questions before we put it all together?"
   - Fallback: FIRST CUT -- compress to 2 min if running long. Show phasor~ briefly, mention +~ mixing, skip detailed multi-voice explanation. Say: "Patch 06 adds modulation and mixing -- the annotations explain everything. Open it at home."

10. **[36:00-39:00] Patch 07: Noise Sculpture Rebuild (3 min)**
    - PATCH: `07-noise-sculpture-rebuild.maxpat` -- show raw patching view (no Presentation Mode)
    - "We just built the entire noise sculpture from scratch. This patch uses every concept from patches 01-06."
    - A/B compare: toggle this patch, then switch to demo in Presentation Mode -- same sound, different view
    - Point out: the demo's polished Presentation Mode hides the patching. "Presentation Mode is the performer's view."
    - Fallback: FIRST CUT -- skip entirely if running long (save 3 min). Jump from Patch 06 to collective finale. Say: "Patch 07 in your download reconstructs the full demo -- study it at home."

11. **[39:00-45:00] Collective Finale and Wrap-Up (6 min)**
    - PATCH: `noise-sculpture-demo.maxpat` -- all students open on their machines
    - Script the setup step by step: "Everyone open noise-sculpture-demo.maxpat. Click Presentation Mode (bottom of window). Click ezdac~. Turn your laptop volume to about 50%."
    - Have students raise hands when they hear sound -- quick visual check
    - Instructor starts first, then on count: "3, 2, 1 -- everyone toggle ON"
    - Let it play for 60-90 seconds. Students can move sliders.
    - "You're hearing 16-18 noise sculptures playing at once. Every one built from the same objects we learned today."
    - Wrap-up: "The patches are yours to study. The annotations explain everything. Next week we go deeper."
    - Fallback: never skip the collective finale. Protect at least 4 minutes for it.

**Format conventions (per CONTEXT.md locked decisions and RESEARCH.md Pattern 1):**

- `**PATCH:**` cue at top of each section with filename + what to highlight

- `> **FALLBACK:**` notes in blockquote format
- `**[TRY THIS - Ns]**` markers with specific student instruction + expected outcome
- `**[CHECKPOINT]**` markers with what to look for
- Pace checkpoint at Patch 04: "If you reach Patch 04 by 25:00, you are on pace"
